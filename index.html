01 / Overview

Customers came for the price.
Give them a reason to stay.

Rankwatch's affordability attracted customers. A difficult experience was contributing to customer loss. The redesign focused on helping people understand their SEO data, even without specialist knowledge.

Challenge

Wider scope

Release

Problem

Design response

Reported signal

Users stayed with the new UI despite a switch-back option.

03 / The brief

Three priorities shaped the direction.

01 / Understand

Make the overview readable

Clear labels, hierarchy and grouped information.

02 / Learn

Explain in context

Guidance beside unfamiliar concepts and opportunities.

03 / Investigate

Preserve the detail

Comparisons and reports for deeper analysis.

Based on the supplied objectives document: accessibility for newcomers, guided workflows, explanatory content and clearer visual hierarchy.

04 / UX decisions

Three directions.
One clearer overview.

From separate numbers to information that belongs together.

01

Exploration / Not selected

A focal point, but scattered comparisons.

Keep: A clear focal point for ranking.

Change: Put keywords and traffic together so comparison requires less looking back and forth.

02

Exploration / Not selected

More trends. More to decode.

Keep: Historical context beside performance data.

Change: Use fewer, larger trend views. Five mini charts compete with the guidance and lack visible scale context here.

05 / The tradeoff

A simpler overview.
The depth stays below.

What the direction gains

A dedicated starting point, related metrics together, and fewer competing charts in the overview.

What it still asks of users

The page remains long. Detailed analysis requires scrolling, and guidance must be specific enough to earn its space.

Design intent: make the first read easier without removing analytical depth.

06 / Consistency at scale

07 / Release & adoption

Customers had a choice.

The new experience launched with an option to return to the old UI. Continued use of the new experience was reported, providing a signal of adoption.

Customer

↗

Switch-back remained available

A 40% improvement was reported, but its metric, baseline and timeframe are unspecified. It is excluded from headline results. Adoption does not establish which individual design choice caused retention.

08 / Takeaway

Help people find meaning
before asking them to explore.

The selected direction brings guidance, grouped metrics and trends into one overview. The next useful validation would measure whether newcomers can identify a meaningful next step, alongside continued product use.
